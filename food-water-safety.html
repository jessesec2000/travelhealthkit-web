Food & Water Safety

Avoid travelers diarrhea and foodborne illness

🔥 The "Boil It, Cook It, Peel It, or Forget It" Rule

This simple rule has protected travelers for decades. When in doubt, apply it strictly.

♨️
Boil It
Boiling water kills bacteria, viruses, and parasites. Let it boil for at least 1 minute (3 minutes at high altitude). Hot beverages like tea and coffee are generally safe if served steaming hot.
🍳
Cook It
Food cooked thoroughly and served steaming hot is usually safe. Avoid lukewarm food. Meat should be cooked all the way through—no pink in the center.
🍊
Peel It
Fruits and vegetables you can peel yourself (bananas, oranges, mangoes, avocados) are safe. The peel protects the edible part from contamination. Wash your hands before peeling.
🚫
Or Forget It
If it doesn't meet the above criteria, skip it. Raw salads, unpeeled fruits, unpasteurized dairy, and raw seafood are high-risk foods in many destinations.
💡 When Exceptions Apply
In developed countries with safe water systems (Western Europe, Japan, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore), tap water and raw vegetables in restaurants are generally safe. Use your judgment based on local infrastructure.
💧 Water Safety
Safe Water Sources
  • ✅ Sealed bottled water from reputable brands
  • ✅ Water boiled for 1 minute (3 min above 6,500ft/2,000m)
  • ✅ Properly treated water (chemical, filtered, UV)
  • ✅ Hot beverages served steaming hot
  • ✅ Factory-sealed canned or bottled drinks
Unsafe Water Sources
  • ❌ Tap water in most developing countries
  • ❌ Well water, river water, lake water
  • ❌ Ice cubes (unless from purified water)
  • ❌ Fountain drinks mixed with local water
  • ❌ Water from decorative fountains
Water Treatment Methods
♨️ Boiling
The most reliable method. Bring water to a rolling boil for at least 1 minute. At altitudes above 6,500 feet (2,000m), boil for 3 minutes.
🧪 Chemical Treatment
Iodine or chlorine tablets kill most pathogens. Follow product instructions for wait times (typically 30 minutes to 4 hours depending on water temperature and clarity). Not effective against Cryptosporidium.
🔍 Filters
Look for filters with pore sizes of 0.1-0.4 microns to remove bacteria and parasites. "Absolute" pore size is more reliable than "nominal." Combine with chemical treatment or UV for viruses.
⚡ UV Purifiers (SteriPen)
Ultraviolet light destroys bacteria, viruses, and parasites. Requires clear water and correct exposure time. Battery-powered and portable. Less effective in cloudy water.
⚠️ Ice & Brushing Teeth
Ice: Avoid ice unless you know it was made from purified water. Freezing doesn't kill pathogens.

Brushing teeth: Use bottled or boiled water for brushing teeth in high-risk areas. Even small amounts of contaminated water can cause illness.
🍽️ Food Safety
Hot Foods
  • ✅ Food served steaming hot is generally safe
  • ✅ Freshly cooked items from busy kitchens
  • ⚠️ Avoid food that's been sitting lukewarm
Street Food Rules
  • ✅ Watch it being cooked in front of you
  • ✅ Choose stalls with high turnover (locals lining up)
  • ✅ Hot, fresh-cooked items only
  • ❌ Pre-cooked food sitting out
  • ❌ Raw or undercooked items
Raw Foods to Avoid
  • ❌ Fresh salads and raw vegetables
  • ❌ Unpeeled fruits (unless you peel them)
  • ❌ Raw or undercooked meat
  • ❌ Raw shellfish (oysters, ceviche, sushi)
  • ❌ Unpasteurized eggs
Dairy Products
  • ✅ Pasteurized milk and cheese
  • ✅ Hard cheeses (even if unpasteurized)
  • ❌ Unpasteurized milk and soft cheeses
  • ❌ Ice cream from questionable sources
Seafood Precautions
  • ✅ Fresh seafood from reputable restaurants
  • ✅ Thoroughly cooked fish and shellfish
  • ⚠️ Check for proper refrigeration
  • ❌ Raw or undercooked seafood
  • ❌ Reef fish in tropical areas (ciguatera risk)
Buffet Dangers
  • ⚠️ Check that hot foods are kept hot (>140°F/60°C)
  • ⚠️ Cold foods should be cold (<40°F/4°C)
  • ❌ Food sitting out at room temperature
  • ❌ Items with questionable freshness
🤢 Travelers Diarrhea

Affects 30-70% of travelers to high-risk destinations. Usually resolves in 3-5 days but can ruin your trip.

Causes
  • ⚠️ Bacteria (80%): E. coli, Salmonella, Campylobacter, Shigella
  • ⚠️ Viruses: Norovirus, Rotavirus
  • ⚠️ Parasites: Giardia, Cryptosporidium (less common, longer lasting)
Prevention
  • ✅ Follow food and water safety rules strictly
  • ✅ Wash hands frequently with soap
  • ✅ Use alcohol-based sanitizer (60%+ alcohol)
  • ✅ Bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol) can reduce risk by 50-65%
Treatment Hierarchy
Follow these steps in order for best recovery
1
Rehydration
Most important! Use oral rehydration salts (ORS), sports drinks, or clean water with a pinch of salt and sugar. Take frequent small sips.
2
Loperamide (Imodium)
Reduces diarrhea for travel days when you can't stay near a bathroom. Do NOT use if you have fever or bloody stool. Use only short-term.
3
Azithromycin (Prescription)
Antibiotic for moderate to severe travelers diarrhea. Ask your doctor before travel. Typically single dose or 3-day course. Effective against most bacterial causes.
🚨 When to See a Doctor
• Blood in stool
• Fever above 101°F (38.5°C)
• Severe abdominal pain
• Signs of dehydration (dizziness, dark urine)
• Symptoms lasting more than 3 days
• More than 6 watery stools per day
🌍 Safe Eating by Region

Risk varies by destination. Adjust your precautions accordingly.

🔴 High Risk
South Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, parts of the Middle East
Apply the "boil it, cook it, peel it, or forget it" rule strictly. Avoid all raw foods and tap water. High incidence of travelers diarrhea (50-70%).
🟡 Moderate Risk
Southeast Asia, Central America, Caribbean, parts of South America
Exercise caution with street food and water. Moderate incidence (20-40%). Be careful outside tourist areas and major cities.
🟢 Lower Risk
East Asia (Japan, South Korea), Eastern Europe, Southern Europe, South Africa, Argentina, Chile
Tap water generally safe in major cities. Standard food safety precautions apply. Lower incidence (8-20%).
💡 Lowest Risk
USA, Canada, Western Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Japan
Food and water standards similar to home. Normal caution sufficient for most travelers.
⏰ The "2-Hour Rule"

Food left at room temperature is when bacteria multiply fastest. This is especially important in hot climates where food spoils even faster.

The Danger Zone: 40°F - 140°F (4°C - 60°C)
Bacteria grow rapidly in this temperature range
  • ❌ Food left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours
  • ❌ In hot weather (above 90°F/32°C), the limit drops to 1 hour
  • ✅ Hot food should be kept above 140°F (60°C)
  • ✅ Cold food should be kept below 40°F (4°C)
⚠️ Common Scenarios
Hotel breakfast buffets: Check how long items have been sitting out. Early risers get the safest food.

Street food: Choose vendors who cook to order, not those with pre-cooked items waiting.

Airline food: Can be risky on long-haul flights if not stored properly.
