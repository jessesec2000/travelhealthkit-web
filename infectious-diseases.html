🦟 Mosquito-Borne Diseases
🦟

Malaria

High Priority

Caused by Plasmodium parasites transmitted through infected mosquito bites. One of the most serious travel-related infections.

Symptoms
Fever, chills, headache
Onset
7 days - 1 year
Vaccine
RTS,S limited
Prophylaxis
Available

⚕️ Prescription Required
Your doctor will determine the right medication and dosage for you based on your health history, destination, and trip duration.

Atovaquone-Proguanil (Malarone): Typically taken daily. Generally well-tolerated with fewer side effects. Your doctor will advise when to start and stop.

Doxycycline: Typically taken daily. Common side effect: sun sensitivity. Your doctor will determine if this is appropriate for you.

Mefloquine (Lariam): Typically taken weekly. Not suitable for everyone due to potential neuropsychiatric side effects. Your doctor will assess your suitability.

Tafenoquine: Newer option with single-dose or weekly regimens. Requires G6PD enzyme testing before use. Your doctor will advise if appropriate.

Sources: CDC Yellow Book 2024, WHO Malaria Guidelines, American Society of Tropical Medicine & Hygiene

🚨
Medical Emergency Malaria can be fatal. Seek immediate care if fever develops during or after travel to endemic areas.
🌡️

Dengue Fever

Common

Viral infection transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes. Found in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. Increasing global spread.

Symptoms
High fever, severe pain
Onset
4-10 days
Vaccine
Dengvaxia (limited)
Treatment
Supportive only
⚠️ No widely available vaccine for travelers
⚠️

Zika Virus

Pregnancy Risk

Flavivirus spread by Aedes mosquitoes. Can cause birth defects if contracted during pregnancy.

Symptoms
Mild fever, rash
Transmission
Mosquito, sexual
Vaccine
None
Treatment
Supportive
🤰
Pregnancy Warning Can cause microcephaly in fetuses. Pregnant women should avoid endemic areas. Partners of pregnant women should use protection for 3 months after return.

Serious viral hemorrhagic disease. Many countries require proof of vaccination for entry.

Symptoms
Fever, jaundice, bleeding
Case Fatality
20-60%
Vaccine
Required

Lifetime

🧠

Japanese Encephalitis

Rural Asia

Viral brain infection transmitted by Culex mosquitoes. Found in rural agricultural areas of Asia and Western Pacific.

Symptoms
Encephalitis, seizures
Case Fatality
~30%
Vaccine
IXIARO available
Risk Areas
Rural Asia

Recommended for:

• Travelers spending >1 month in endemic areas during transmission season

• Short-term travelers to rural areas with outdoor exposure

• Travelers to areas with current outbreaks

• Lab workers with potential exposure

Chikungunya

Debilitating Pain

Viral disease causing severe joint pain. Transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes. Found in Africa, Asia, Americas.

Symptoms
Severe joint pain, fever
Duration
Weeks to years
Vaccine
Ixchiq (new)
Treatment
Pain management
✅ Ixchiq vaccine now available (18+ years)

🛡️ Mosquito Prevention Methods
  • ✓
    DEET 20-50%
    Apply to exposed skin. Reapply after swimming/sweating. Safe for pregnant women and children >2 months.
  • ✓
    Permethrin-Treated Clothing
    Treat clothes, bed nets, and gear. Kills mosquitoes on contact. Do not apply to skin.
  • ✓
    Insecticide-Treated Bed Nets
    Essential for malaria zones. Ensure no holes. Tuck under mattress.
  • ✓
    Peak Hours Awareness
    Anopheles (malaria): Dusk to dawn
    Aedes (dengue/Zika): Daytime
  • ✓
    Protective Clothing
    Long sleeves, long pants, light colors. Loose-fitting clothing harder for mosquitoes to bite through.
Other Vector-Borne
🦌

Lyme Disease

Tick-Borne

Bacterial infection transmitted by Ixodes ticks. Found in temperate regions: Northeastern USA, Europe, Asia.

Symptoms
Bullseye rash, fatigue
Onset
3-30 days
Vaccine
None
Treatment
Antibiotics

Before outdoor activities:

• Use DEET on skin, permethrin on clothing

• Wear long pants tucked into socks

• Stay on trails, avoid tall grass/brush

After exposure:

• Shower within 2 hours

• Do full-body tick check

• Remove ticks promptly with tweezers

🏜️

Leishmaniasis

Sandfly

Parasitic disease transmitted by sandflies. Cutaneous (skin sores) and visceral (internal organ) forms exist.

Symptoms
Skin sores, fever
Risk Areas
Tropics, Mediterranean
Vaccine
None
Prevention
Avoid sandfly bites
🦟
Sandfly Behavior Tiny insects active from dusk to dawn. Smaller than mosquitoes - bed nets with smaller mesh recommended.
💤

African Sleeping Sickness

Tsetse Fly

Trypanosomiasis caused by protozoan parasites. Transmitted by tsetse flies in sub-Saharan Africa. Can be fatal if untreated.

Symptoms
Fever, sleep disruption
Risk Areas
Rural sub-Saharan Africa
Vaccine
None
Treatment
Complex drugs

Challenging to prevent:

• Tsetse flies are not repelled by DEET

• Wear neutral-colored clothing (avoid bright/dark)

• Long sleeves and pants essential

• Avoid bushy areas during day (peak activity)

• Vehicle screens if traveling through risk areas

Water & Food-Borne
🥘

Typhoid Fever

Contaminated Food/Water

Bacterial infection caused by Salmonella typhi. Common in areas with poor sanitation. Can be life-threatening.

Symptoms
High fever, GI symptoms
Transmission
Fecal-oral route
Vaccine
Oral or injectable
Treatment
Antibiotics

Vi Polysaccharide (injectable):

• Single dose, 2 weeks before travel

• Protection lasts 2 years

• Approved for age 2+

Ty21a Live Oral:

• 4 capsules over 7 days

• Protection lasts 5 years

• Not for immunocompromised

🫁

Hepatitis A & E

Fecal-Oral

Viral liver infections. Hep A common worldwide in areas with poor sanitation. Hep E common in South Asia, similar transmission.

Hep A Vaccine
Highly effective
Hep E Vaccine
China only
Symptoms
Jaundice, fatigue
Risk
Shellfish, produce
✅ Hep A vaccine recommended for most travelers
💧

Cholera

Water-Borne

Bacterial infection causing severe watery diarrhea. Rapid dehydration can be fatal within hours. Associated with poor sanitation and contaminated water.

Symptoms
Rice-water stools
Transmission
Contaminated water
Vaccine
Oral (Dukoral)
Treatment
Rehydration critical

Safe water strategies:

• Boil water for 1 minute (3 min at altitude)

• Water purification tablets (iodine/chlorine)

• UV sterilizers (SteriPEN)

• Filters with 0.1-0.4 micron pores

• Bottled water from sealed containers

• Avoid ice, uncooked vegetables, unpeeled fruit

🗺️ High Risk Destinations
🌍 Sub-Saharan Africa
▼
Malaria Yellow Fever Typhoid Sleeping Sickness

Key concerns: Malaria prophylaxis essential throughout. Yellow fever vaccine required for entry to many countries. Sleeping sickness in rural areas with tsetse flies. High burden of water-borne diseases.

🏯 South & Southeast Asia
▼
Malaria Dengue Japanese Encephalitis Cholera Hepatitis E

Key concerns: Japanese encephalitis risk in rural agricultural areas. Cholera outbreaks periodic. Hepatitis E more common than other regions. Dengue endemic in urban and rural areas. Drug-resistant malaria in Greater Mekong.

🌎 Central & South America
▼
Malaria Yellow Fever Dengue Chikungunya Zika Leishmaniasis

Key concerns: Yellow fever in Amazon basin and parts of Panama. Malaria in rural areas of Amazon, Central America. Zika risk remains in many areas - pregnancy caution. Cutaneous leishmaniasis in forested areas.

🏝️ Caribbean
▼
Dengue Chikungunya Zika Malaria (Hispaniola)

Key concerns: Dengue and chikungunya widespread. Zika risk present - caution for pregnant travelers. Malaria only on Hispaniola (Haiti/Dominican Republic). Yellow fever not endemic but vaccine may be required.

🐠 Oceania & Pacific Islands
▼
Malaria (PNG, Solomons) Dengue Japanese Encephalitis Chikungunya

Key concerns: Malaria in Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands, Vanuatu. Japanese encephalitis in parts of Australia (Torres Strait), Papua New Guinea. Dengue outbreaks common in Pacific islands.

🌲 Temperate Regions (USA, Europe, Asia)
▼
Lyme Disease Leishmaniasis Tick-borne Encephalitis

Key concerns: Lyme disease risk in northeastern USA, Europe, forested areas of Asia. Tick-borne encephalitis vaccine available for Central/Eastern Europe and Russia. Mediterranean leishmaniasis in Southern Europe.

✈️ Pre-Travel Checklist
  • ✓
    Visit travel clinic 4-6 weeks before
    Some vaccines require multiple doses or take time to become effective
  • ✓
    Get destination-specific vaccines
    Yellow fever, typhoid, hepatitis A, Japanese encephalitis as indicated
  • ✓
    Pack malaria prophylaxis if needed
    Start before departure as directed by your provider
  • ✓
    Bring insect repellent and bed net
    DEET 20-50%, permethrin spray for clothing, insecticide-treated net
  • ✓
    Pack water treatment supplies
    Purification tablets, filter, or plan to buy bottled water
