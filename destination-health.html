Water & Food Safety
💧

Drink Only Safe Water

Bottled, boiled, or purified water only. Use purification tablets or filters for trekking. Avoid ice cubes.

🍽️

Eat Cooked Foods

"Boil it, cook it, peel it, or forget it." Avoid raw vegetables, salads, and unpeeled fruits.

🥛

Avoid Dairy

Unpasteurized milk and dairy products are risky. Only consume if from trusted sources.

🍵

Dal Bhat Power

The traditional meal (rice, lentils, vegetables) is generally safe and provides good energy for trekking.

Altitude Guidelines
⛰️ Trekking Safely
📈

Golden Rule

Above 3,000m: Sleep no more than 300-500m higher per day. Include acclimatization days every 3-4 days.

🌿

Diamox (Acetazolamide)

Can help prevent AMS. Discuss with your doctor before the trip. Start 1-2 days before ascent.

⚠️

Descend Immediately If

Severe headache, confusion, difficulty walking, or shortness of breath at rest. AMS can be fatal.

Water & Food Safety
💧

Stick to Bottled Water

Tap water is not safe to drink. Use bottled water even for brushing teeth. Ice is generally safe (made from purified water).

🍜

Street Food Smarts

Choose stalls with high turnover, fresh ingredients, and food cooked to order. Avoid pre-cooked food sitting at room temperature.

🌶️

Spice Levels

Thai food is very spicy. Say "mai pet" (not spicy) or "pet nit noi" (a little spicy). Build up tolerance gradually.

🥗

Raw Foods Caution

Avoid raw vegetables, salads, and unpeeled fruits. Som tam (papaya salad) is often made with raw vegetables—eat with caution.

Water & Food Safety
💧

No Tap Water

Tap water is unsafe throughout Peru. Use bottled or purified water. Avoid ice unless made from purified water.

🥘

Ceviche Caution

Peru's famous ceviche is raw fish. Only eat at reputable restaurants with high turnover. Avoid street ceviche.

🍵

Coca Tea for Altitude

Coca tea (mate de coca) is legal and commonly offered for altitude sickness. Mildly effective but not a cure.

🧼

Hand Hygiene

Wash hands frequently. Carry hand sanitizer. Food safety standards vary outside tourist areas.

Altitude Guidelines
⛰️ Highlands Travel
📈

Acclimatization

Spend 2-3 days in the Sacred Valley (lower altitude) before Cusco. Drink plenty of water, avoid alcohol initially.

💊

Diamox

Consider prophylactic Diamox if flying directly to Cusco. Start 24 hours before ascent. Discuss with doctor.

⚠️

Severe Symptoms

If you experience confusion, difficulty walking straight, or breathlessness at rest—DESCEND IMMEDIATELY.

Safari Health Tips
🦁 Wildlife & Health
🦟

Mosquito Protection

Use DEET 20-30%, wear long sleeves/pants at dawn/dusk. Sleep under mosquito nets provided by camps.

☀️

Sun Protection

Equatorial sun is intense. SPF 50+, hat, sunglasses essential. Game drives mean long sun exposure.

💊

Complete Prophylaxis

Take malaria pills exactly as prescribed. Start before arrival, continue after departure. Don't skip doses.

Water & Food Safety
💧

Tap Water is Safe

Tap water is generally safe in most areas including San José. In very remote areas, use bottled water as precaution.

🍽️

Casado & Local Food

Traditional "casado" (rice, beans, meat, salad) is safe and nutritious. Food safety standards are decent in tourist areas.

Fresh Produce

🧉

"Pura Vida" Drinks

Fresh fruit juices are popular. Ensure they're made with purified water at reputable establishments.

Adventure Activity Safety

Rip Currents

Water & Food Safety
💧

NO Tap Water - Ever

Use only bottled water from sealed bottles. Brush teeth with bottled water. No ice unless from purified water.

🥗

Avoid Raw Everything

No salads, raw vegetables, or unpeeled fruits. Chutneys and sauces may be made with tap water. Eat only piping hot food.

🥛

Dairy Dangers

Lassi (yogurt drink) can be risky if made with tap water or unpasteurized milk. Only drink packaged/bottled lassi.

🌶️

Spice Strategy

Indian food is very spicy. Start mild. Spicy food + contaminated oil = worse stomach issues. Ask for "less spicy" or "no chili."

🍵

Tea Safety

Chai is usually safe (boiled milk/water). But ask for "fresh cup" as reused cups may be rinsed in tap water.
