Pediatric Travel Medicine

Keeping little travelers safe and healthy on the road

⚠️ Important
Children are not small adults. They have different risks, medication dosages, and vaccination schedules. Always consult a pediatric travel medicine specialist before traveling with children.
Travel by Age
👶 Infants (0-12 months)

Immune systems still developing. Many vaccines not yet given. Risk of dehydration is high.

⚠️ Avoid travel before 2-3 months (first vaccines)
🧸 Toddlers (1-3 years)

Mobile but unaware of dangers. High risk from contaminated surfaces (hand-to-mouth behavior).

⚠️ Extra vigilance with hygiene and supervision
🎒 School Age (4-11 years)

Better at following instructions but still vulnerable. Can take most adult medications (adjusted dose).

✓ Good age for educational travel with proper prep
🧑 Adolescents (12+ years)

Can take adult doses of most medications. Risk-taking behavior increases.

⚠️ Discuss safety and emergency protocols

Child-Specific Risks
🌡️

Heat Exhaustion

Children heat up faster and sweat less. Offer frequent fluids, rest in shade, and watch for irritability or lethargy.

💧

Dehydration

Smaller fluid reserves. Diarrhea becomes dangerous quickly. Use oral rehydration salts (ORS) at first sign.

☀️

Sunburn

Thinner, more sensitive skin. Use SPF 50+, protective clothing. Avoid sun 10am-4pm.

🦟

Mosquito-Borne Illness

Malaria, dengue, Zika more severe in children. Use bed nets, repellent (age-appropriate), and cover limbs.

🍽️

Foodborne Illness

Less stomach acid to kill bacteria. Breastfeeding infants when possible. Pack familiar snacks.

✈️ Flying with Car Seats
✓

Bring Your Own

Most airlines allow FAA-approved car seats for children under 2. Check with your airline first.

💡

Alternative: CARES Harness

FAA-approved harness for toddlers 22-44 lbs. Much lighter than a car seat.

⚠️

Check Local Availability

Car seats may not be available for rental at your destination, or quality may vary.

Keeping Kids Safe
🎒

Pack a "Go Bag"

Include snacks, water, wipes, small toys, and a change of clothes. Always accessible!

👀

Constant Supervision

Unfamiliar environments = new hazards. No running near pools. Check balconies/windows in hotels.

📱

Emergency Contact Card

Put your hotel address and phone number in child's pocket. Teach them to show it if lost.

💊

Child-Proof Medications

Keep all meds in original containers. Store out of reach. Don't call medicine "candy."

🚰

Safe Water Everywhere

Bring a portable water purifier. Kids drink from any cup they find—be vigilant!

When to NOT Travel
🚫

Before 2-3 Months Old

Before first round of vaccinations. Immune system too vulnerable. Higher risk on planes.

🚫

Active Infections / High Fever

Never travel with a sick child (except to seek medical care). Risk to child and others.

🚫

High Altitude with Young Children

Children under 2 years should not travel above 2,500m/8,200ft. Risk of altitude sickness.

🚫

Remote Areas Without Medical Care

If more than 24h from pediatric-capable hospital, reconsider with young children.

🚫

During Disease Outbreaks

Children are often more vulnerable to emerging infectious diseases.

Pre-Travel Checklist
  • Pediatric travel medicine consultation
  • Update routine vaccinations (MMR, DTaP, etc.)
  • Get travel-specific vaccines
  • Pack child-safe insect repellent
  • Bring travel crib/bed rails if needed
  • Pack extra formula/food (familiar brands)
  • Child-specific first aid kit
  • Copy of vaccination records
