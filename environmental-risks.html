Environmental Risks

Stay safe in extreme environments

🏔️ Altitude Risks
Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) Mild

Most common altitude illness. Symptoms typically appear 6-12 hours after ascent.

  • Headache
  • Nausea/vomiting
  • Fatigue, dizziness
  • Loss of appetite
  • Poor sleep
High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE) Life-Threatening

Fluid accumulation in the lungs. Can be fatal within hours.

  • Shortness of breath at rest
  • Tightness in chest
  • Persistent cough (may produce frothy sputum)
  • Extreme fatigue/weakness
  • Blue lips/fingernails (cyanosis)
High Altitude Cerebral Edema (HACE) Life-Threatening

Brain swelling from fluid leakage. Medical emergency requiring immediate descent.

  • Severe headache (not relieved by medication)
  • Confusion, altered mental state
  • Difficulty walking (ataxia)
  • Behavioral changes
  • Loss of consciousness
⚠️ When to Turn Around
STOP ascent immediately if symptoms worsen. DESCENT is the only cure for HAPE/HACE. Never sleep higher if symptomatic. Descend at least 500-1000m (1,500-3,000 ft).
🛡️ Prevention
  • Gradual ascent: Above 2,500m (8,000 ft), increase sleeping altitude by max 300-500m (1,000-1,500 ft) per day
  • Rest days: Take a rest day every 3-4 days or 1,000m (3,000 ft) gained
  • "Climb high, sleep low" when possible
  • Diamox (Acetazolamide): 125-250mg twice daily, starting 1 day before ascent
  • Avoid alcohol and sedatives
  • Stay hydrated but don't overhydrate
🚑 Treatment
  • Immediate descent - most important action
  • Supplemental oxygen if available
  • Dexamethasone for HACE: 4mg every 6 hours
  • Nifedipine for HAPE: 30mg slow-release every 12 hours
  • Portable hyperbaric chamber (Gamow bag) if descent not immediately possible
☀️ Heat & Sun
Heat Exhaustion vs Heat Stroke

Know the difference - one is serious, the other is deadly.

Heat Exhaustion
  • Heavy sweating
  • Cool skin
  • Muscle cramps
  • Fatigue, weakness
  • Nausea
  • Normal mental state
Heat Stroke ⚠️
  • NO sweating (hot, dry skin)
  • Body temp >40°C (104°F)
  • Confusion, seizures
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Rapid pulse
  • MEDICAL EMERGENCY
Heat Stroke Response
Call emergency services immediately. Cool rapidly: move to shade, remove clothing, apply ice packs to neck/armpits/groin, immerse in cool water if possible. Minutes matter.
Sunburn Prevention Prevention
  • SPF 30+ minimum (SPF 50+ for extended exposure)
  • Reapply every 2 hours - more often if swimming/sweating
  • Apply 15-30 minutes before sun exposure
  • Use 1oz (shot glass) for full body coverage
  • Don't forget ears, neck, feet, lips (SPF lip balm)
  • Seek shade 10am-4pm when UV is strongest
  • UPF-rated clothing more effective than sunscreen
Dehydration Risks
  • Thirst is a late indicator - drink before thirsty
  • Urine should be pale yellow (not dark)
  • Increase fluid intake in heat: 2-4L per day minimum
  • Electrolyte replacement for heavy sweating (>1 hour activity)
  • Avoid alcohol and excessive caffeine
  • Children and elderly at higher risk
📊 UV Index Awareness
  • 0-2: Low - minimal protection needed
  • 3-5: Moderate - seek shade midday, wear sunscreen
  • 6-7: High - protection essential, reduce sun 10am-4pm
  • 8-10: Very High - extra protection, minimize sun exposure
  • 11+: Extreme - avoid sun exposure, full protection mandatory

UV index is higher at altitude, near equator, and near reflective surfaces (snow, water, sand).

🌡️ Heat Index Concept

"Feels like" temperature combining air temperature + humidity. High humidity impairs body's ability to cool through sweating.

  • 27-32°C (80-90°F): Caution - fatigue possible
  • 32-41°C (90-105°F): Extreme caution - heat cramps/exhaustion possible
  • 41-54°C (105-130°F): Danger - heat stroke likely with prolonged exposure
  • 54°C+ (130°F+): Extreme danger - heat stroke imminent
❄️ Cold & Hypothermia
Frostbite Stages

Freezing of skin and underlying tissues. Most common in fingers, toes, ears, nose, cheeks.

Stage 1: Frostnip
  • Numb, pale skin
  • Soft to touch
  • Reversible with rewarming - no permanent damage
  • Treatment: Gentle rewarming, warm water (37-39°C / 99-102°F)
Stage 2: Superficial Frostbite
  • Skin feels warm (paradoxical)
  • Red/white mottled appearance
  • Blistering may occur after rewarming
  • Medical attention recommended
Stage 3: Deep/Severe Frostbite ⚠️
  • Skin white, waxy, or gray-yellow
  • Feels hard, wooden to touch
  • Tissue death risk - potential amputation
  • Medical emergency
Hypothermia Life-Threatening

Core body temperature drops below 35°C (95°F). Can occur even in mild weather with wind/wet.

Remember the "Umbles"
Stumbles (clumsy, poor coordination)
Mumbles (slurred speech)
Fumbles (difficulty with fine motor)
Grumbles (confusion, personality changes)
  • Mild: Shivering, cold hands/feet, alert
  • Moderate: Violent shivering, confusion, drowsy
  • Severe: No shivering, unconscious, rigid muscles
Wind Chill Factor

Wind strips away the thin layer of warm air next to skin. Danger increases dramatically with wind.

  • Frostbite can occur in minutes at high wind chill
  • 30+ mph wind can make 0°F feel like -26°F
  • Exposed skin at highest risk
  • Check wind chill charts before exposure
Layering System
1
Base Layer: Moisture-wicking (synthetic/merino) - NO cotton
2
Insulating Layer: Traps heat (fleece, down, synthetic)
3
Shell Layer: Wind/water protection (breathable waterproof)
⚠️ Stay Dry Principle
Wet clothing loses 90% of insulating value. Sweat, rain, or snow can cause hypothermia even in temperatures above freezing. Change out of wet clothes immediately.
⚠️ Environmental Hazards
Air Pollution (AQI)
  • 0-50 (Green): Good - no precautions needed
  • 51-100 (Yellow): Moderate - sensitive groups limit outdoor exertion
  • 101-150 (Orange): Unhealthy for sensitive groups
  • 151-200 (Red): Unhealthy - everyone limit prolonged exertion
  • 201-300 (Purple): Very unhealthy - avoid outdoor exertion
  • 301+ (Maroon): Hazardous - remain indoors
Protection
  • N95 masks filter PM2.5 particles
  • Exercise indoors on high AQI days
  • Close windows, use air purifiers
  • Check AQI apps before outdoor activities
Altitude UV & Snow Blindness

UV increases ~10% per 1,000m altitude. Snow reflects up to 80% of UV.

  • Snow blindness: Sunburn of the cornea (photokeratitis)
  • Symptoms appear 6-12 hours after exposure
  • Severe pain, gritty sensation, light sensitivity, tearing
  • Usually resolves in 24-48 hours but very painful
  • Prevention: Glacier goggles/sunglasses with side shields (CAT 3-4)
Natural Disasters
🌊 Tsunami Awareness
  • Feel strong earthquake? Move to high ground immediately
  • Tsunami can arrive within minutes
  • Don't wait for official warning
  • First wave may not be largest
  • Stay away from coast for 24+ hours
🌋 Volcano Awareness
  • Check volcanic activity before travel
  • Carry N95 mask for ash (regular masks ineffective)
  • Ash can damage lungs, eyes, vehicles, electronics
  • Stay indoors during heavy ashfall
  • Clear roof ash (can cause collapse)
🌍 Earthquake Safety
  • Drop, Cover, Hold On
  • Get under sturdy furniture, cover head/neck
  • Stay away from windows, heavy objects
  • If outdoors: move to open area away from buildings
  • Know tsunami risk if near coast
Wildlife Encounters
🐻 Bears
  • Black bears: Fight back if attacked
  • Grizzly bears: Play dead (face down, hands behind neck)
  • Make noise while hiking (don't surprise them)
  • Carry bear spray, know how to use it
  • Store food properly (bear canisters/hangs)
  • Never run - triggers chase response
🐍 Snakes
  • Most bites occur when trying to handle/kill snake
  • Watch where you step/put hands (rock crevices, wood piles)
  • Wear boots and long pants in snake habitat
  • If bitten: keep calm, immobilize, seek medical help
  • Don't cut, suck, or apply ice/tourniquet
🦟 Insects
  • DEET 20-30% or picaridin for mosquitoes/ticks
  • Permethrin-treated clothing for tick areas
  • Check for ticks daily (prompt removal reduces disease risk)
  • Use mosquito nets where malaria/dengue present
Poisonous Plants
"Leaves of Three, Let It Be"
Applies to poison ivy, oak, and sumac. All contain urushiol oil that causes allergic contact dermatitis.
  • Poison Ivy: Climbing vine or shrub, pointed leaflets
  • Poison Oak: Shrub or vine, lobed leaves resembling oak
  • Poison Sumac: Woody shrub, 7-13 leaflets per stem
  • Oil can remain active on clothing/pets/tools for years
  • Rash appears 12-72 hours after exposure
  • Wash with soap and water within 15 minutes to prevent/minimize rash
  • Calamine lotion, cool compresses, antihistamines for symptoms
  • Seek medical care if widespread or on face/genitals
🌊 Water Environments
Rip Currents Leading Cause of Beach Rescues

Strong, narrow channels of water flowing away from shore. Can exceed 8 feet/second - faster than Olympic swimmers.

🌊 How to Escape
1. DON'T PANIC - Stay calm, you will float
2. DON'T FIGHT DIRECTLY - Swimming straight to shore exhausts you
3. SWIM PARALLEL - Swim parallel to shore until out of current
4. THEN ANGLE IN - Once free, swim at angle back to shore
5. FLOAT IF TIRED - Tread water or float, signal for help
  • Look for: channel of churning water, line of foam/seaweed, darker water, gap in waves
  • Always swim near lifeguards
  • If you see someone caught: throw flotation, don't become second victim
Jellyfish Stings
❌ Myth
Pee on the sting to neutralize venom
✅ Fact
Urine can actually worsen the sting. Use vinegar for box jellyfish, or hot water (not scalding) for most other species.
  • Rinse with seawater (NOT freshwater - triggers more stingers)
  • Remove tentacles with tweezers (not fingers)
  • Hot water immersion (40-45°C / 104-113°F) for 20 minutes for pain
  • Box jellyfish (Australia/Indo-Pacific): vinegar first, then seek emergency care
  • Portuguese man o' war: different species - hot water, not vinegar
  • Seek immediate help for breathing difficulty, chest pain, or widespread sting
Coral Cuts

Coral polyps leave foreign protein and calcium carbonate in wounds. High infection risk.

  • Immediate: Flush vigorously with clean water
  • Scrub gently with soap and brush to remove debris
  • Rinse with half-strength hydrogen peroxide
  • Apply antiseptic and cover with sterile dressing
  • Monitor for infection: increasing redness, warmth, pus, red streaks
  • Seek medical care - may need antibiotics or foreign body removal
  • Watch for coral poisoning (coral dermatitis): red, itchy, blistering rash
⚠️ Don't Touch Coral
Coral cuts take weeks to heal, easily infected, and damage fragile ecosystems. Look but don't touch.
Marine Animal Hazards
🐡 Venomous Fish
  • Stonefish, scorpionfish, lionfish: venomous spines
  • Hot water immersion (as hot as tolerable) denatures venom
  • Seek medical care - antivenom exists for stonefish
🐠 Stingrays
  • Shuffle feet when wading to avoid stepping on them
  • If stung: hot water immersion, seek medical care
  • Barb can break off - may need surgical removal
🦈 Sharks
  • Shark attacks rare (more deaths from vending machines)
  • Avoid dawn/dusk swimming, murky water, areas with seals/bait fish
  • Don't wear shiny jewelry, don't splash excessively
  • If attacked: target eyes and gills aggressively
🐙 Blue-Ringed Octopus & Cone Snails
  • Highly venomous - found Indo-Pacific region
  • No antivenom available
  • Any bite = medical emergency - may need CPR/ventilation support
  • Don't pick up shells or touch small octopuses
